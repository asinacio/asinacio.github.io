Research

My research focuses on experimental neutrino physics, with particular interests in low-energy neutrinos, neutrinoless double-beta decay searches, and the development of liquid-scintillator detectors.

Low-Energy Neutrino Physics

Solar neutrinos provide a unique opportunity to study both the properties of neutrinos and the processes taking place in the interior of the Sun. Their detection has played a central role in establishing neutrino flavour oscillations and continues to provide important tests of neutrino physics and solar models.

My interests include the study of low-energy solar neutrinos with liquid-scintillator detectors, with a particular focus on extracting neutrino signals from challenging backgrounds and improving the sensitivity of future measurements. Additionally, I also want to use solar neutrinos to look for exotic phenomena, like solar antineutrinos, that could reveal new neutrino interactions or physics beyond the Standard Model.

Neutrinoless Double-Beta Decay

Neutrinoless double-beta decay is one of the most sensitive probes of the fundamental nature of the neutrino. Its observation would demonstrate that lepton number is not conserved, provide evidence that neutrinos are Majorana particles, and offer insight into the origin and value of the neutrino masses.

The search for this extremely rare process requires a detailed characterisation of the relevant backgrounds and a thorough understanding of the detector. I have worked on both aspects within the SNO+ experiment, contributing to the study and modelling of backgrounds as well as to detector calibration and performance studies.
