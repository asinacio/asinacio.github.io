Science communication is one of the parts of being a physicist that I find most rewarding. I enjoy finding different ways to make difficult ideas accessible, whether I am speaking with a scientist, teaching a classroom of students, engaging with the general public, or exploring physics through a hands-on activity with children.

Some outreach I've done over the years

  • Volunteer at the University of Oxford Open Doors day, introducing visitors to experimental particle physics and the research carried out in the Physics Department.
  • Hands-on activities introducing younger audiences to physics, from a Crater Art workshop at the University of Oxford to particle physics at a community stargazing event in Oxford.
  • Guest at the Royal Society STEM Research Careers Event organised with In2scienceUK, supporting young people from disadvantaged backgrounds in exploring STEM careers.
  • Correspondent in Cartas com Ciência, a programme connecting scientists and students in Portuguese-speaking countries through an exchange of letters.
  • Participated in public sessions for secondary-school students at LIP-Lisbon, including Partículas: do Universo ao Laboratório and Das profundezas da Terra ao Espaço.

I am also interested in communicating science through formats that are a little less traditional than lectures. Over the years, this has included digital content, social media, and documentary work.

I am always interested in opportunities to talk about physics, visit schools, contribute to public-engagement activities, or work on new ways of communicating science.
