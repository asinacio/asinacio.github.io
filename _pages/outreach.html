I like the moment when a physics question stops sounding like a physics question.

Science communication is one of the parts of being a physicist that I find most rewarding. I enjoy finding different ways into difficult ideas, whether that means a conversation with a scientist, a classroom full of students, or a hands-on activity with children.

My outreach has included public events, school visits, STEM-career activities, science communication projects, and events designed to connect young people with scientists and with the possibilities of a career in science.

01 Why I do it

Physics is much more interesting when people can ask questions about it.

I do not think outreach is just about simplifying difficult ideas. Some of the best conversations start when you let people ask the questions that scientists are not always asked in a lecture hall.

I particularly enjoy outreach that is interactive: giving people something to look at, think about, measure, argue with, or try for themselves. My aim is not necessarily to turn everyone into a physicist, but to leave people feeling that physics is something they can engage with.

04 Science communication

Physics does not have to stay inside the paper.

I am interested in the different ways scientists can communicate beyond traditional academic publications. This has included public events, digital communication, documentary work, and activities designed to make science more accessible to different audiences.

From 2018 to 2021, I worked as Social Media Manager for the LIP Education, Communication and Outreach Group, creating and disseminating quizzes, videos, images, and information about scientific events and initiatives.
