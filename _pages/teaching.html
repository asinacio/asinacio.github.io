I like teaching physics where the answer isn't immediately obvious.

My teaching spans tutorials, experimental laboratories, and research supervision. Across all three, I try to give students the tools to work through a problem rather than simply showing them how it is solved.

01 How I teach
01

Problem solving

My tutorial teaching focuses on problem-solving and guided discussion rather than simply working through solutions. I want students to become comfortable with taking a problem apart, deciding what matters, and finding a way forward.

02

Learning by doing

In laboratory teaching, I particularly enjoy helping students move from an experiment that does not quite work to understanding why. This means thinking about the apparatus, the data, and the assumptions behind the measurement.

03

Research starts early

I enjoy introducing students to research through problems that do not have a worked solution waiting at the end. My student projects draw on real questions from neutrino physics, detector calibration, and experimental data analysis.

03 Teaching through research

Sometimes the best teaching problem is one we don't already know the answer to.

I like introducing students to research through problems drawn from real experiments. I have proposed and supervised projects using SNO+ data and detector systems, giving students experience with the kinds of questions, tools, and analysis methods used in large experimental collaborations.

01

04 Mentoring

Learning how to do research by doing research.

I mentor undergraduate, Master's, PhD, and visiting students working in experimental neutrino physics.

This has included training in neutrino physics, data analysis, detector calibration, simulation, background modelling, and low-background experimental techniques.

I have also provided day-to-day scientific guidance on research projects, from defining the problem and learning the necessary tools through to interpreting results and presenting them to a scientific audience.
